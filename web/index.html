      How it works

      I wrote a simplified D&D 5e fight simulator and played over 3 million fights across 35,000 random encounters (parties of 3 to 6 heroes at levels 1 to 10, monsters from the open SRD). Heroes are built from the 13 classes: hit points, armour, attacks and damage grow with the level, and casters spend spell slots (area spells from level 3, Healing Word and Cure Wounds, smites) while fighters use Action Surge and Second Wind, paladins Lay on Hands and barbarians Rage. Monsters use breath weapons and legendary actions. Heroes can fall and be healed back up, and about a third of the parties start the fight hurt or low on slots. A third of the encounters were picked on purpose from fights that could go either way, and each of those was played many times, because close fights are the hard cases. Each encounter was turned into 52 numbers, such as total hit points, damage per round, spell slots left, breath and legendary damage, the weakest hero and the outcome of a quick dice-free average fight. Three boosted-tree models learned to predict the win chance, the share of party hit points lost, and how many heroes fall.

      The model is a few thousand small decision trees and runs here in your browser. The "Simulation" column is the same simulator running live, so you can see how close the model is. Read the full write-up.

      Limits. The reference answers come from my own simplified simulator, not from real tables: no magic items, no way to switch off regeneration, no control or buff spells, positioning, terrain, resistances or death saves, and every hero plays a simple fixed tactic. Treat the numbers as a sanity check for planning, not a promise.
